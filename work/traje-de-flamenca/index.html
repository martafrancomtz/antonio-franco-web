Traje de flamenca

The traditional dress of the Seville feria.

2025Own patternCotton poplin

The brief

My mother was going to the April feria in Seville and asked me for a dress. I had never built anything with this much volume, and I wanted to draft it from a blank sheet instead of buying a pattern.

The pattern

Own pattern, drafted on her measurements: a close bodice that follows the body down to the knee and only then opens into the skirt.

The body is cut in six panels, so the fit holds through the hip without a single dart. Below the knee there are three tiers of ruffles, each cut as a full circle rather than a gathered strip, so they fall in waves instead of bunching. The lowest tier alone had almost fourteen metres of edge to hem; the whole dress used around nine metres of cotton poplin.

What went wrong

At the first fitting the ruffles pulled the whole skirt down. The back dropped almost three centimetres and the knee line sat too low: the weight of the tiers was hanging from the cloth itself. I stitched a cotton tape stay along the knee seam to carry the load, shortened the radius of the lowest tier to take weight out, and marked the hem again with her standing in the shoes she would wear.

What I learned

To design for weight and movement, not for a shape standing still. Before I cut anything now, I ask where the load goes and what is going to hold it.
