Bag — study after Mulberry

2024Study afterLeather

The brief

I wanted to know whether what I had learned on garments would carry over to a rigid object. I chose the Mulberry Clipper holdall because its shape depends entirely on construction: panels, gussets and a base that has to stand on its own.

The pattern

A bag does not drape, so there was no body to take the pattern from. I measured the original from photographs, scaled it from the width of the strap, and built every panel in card first to check that the gussets closed. The material decided the rest: black cowhide for the body, a thicker tan leather for the base, and a board insert so it keeps its shape when it is empty.

What went wrong

Leatherwork is not sewing. I had to learn edge finishing, skiving and where reinforcements go before any hardware goes on. The first time, I set the strap rings through a single layer of leather, and within a week the holes had started to stretch. I took the tabs off, added a reinforcement under each ring and sewed them again by hand with waxed thread.

What I learned

The crossbody bag was built on intuition; this one was planned before the first cut. In 2023 I solved problems as they appeared. Here I solved most of them in card, before the leather was touched.
